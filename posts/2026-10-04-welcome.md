Inspired by [Jeff Attwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Atwood)'s hugely successful blog [](codinghorror.com) I created a fairly janky Wordpress site believing that I had something interesting to say too.  I should cut myself some slack, it was 2006 social media didn't really exist (at least as we know it today) and blogging was a thing.   

It felt powerful, I had a voice, but something became clear to me when I was doing it, which is my _motivations_ for writing a blog were unclear.   I had originally thought of it as a journal of my technical challenges - because I'd forgotten so much of what I had learnt up until 2006 (full disclosure: my career started in 1994).   But documenting that kind of detail quickly fell by the wayside, and here's the thing, I never actually needed to refer to the work that I had done.   I sort of remembered enough of what I had done, that when it came time to apply my knowledge to something else I would look up what I wanted to know from fresh.   So that purpose wasn't really real.   

But I didn't write much of those kind of things anyway, I spent more time trying to find something interesting to say.  I did that for a year or so.  It's possible I might have said something interesting, I'm not sure, chances are probably not. But at the same time the rise of social media troubled me, I really didn't like it (guess I might be more neurodiverse than I had realised at the time) and made me convinced that the world would get along fine without me giving my opinion.

Why didn't I like social media?  Privacy.  I don't think I was alone in feeling a deep discomfort about what big tech might do with my data in the early days, but i quit out.  Early doors.  In 2007 I closed my Facebook account (having posted exactly zero times) the reason I gave for closing the account? "I want my face back".  I was proud of that rebuke, I'm certain exactly zero people read it.  My twitter account expired with zero tweets.   LinkedIn?  Yeah I keep that going, you never know when you might need a job, but no posts and very rarely read.

I went through a career crisis in 2018, I had become bored with dev.  I'd done it for over 20 years, but it's emotionally stunting.  You write code, that's your job, I wanted to talk to people.   So I swapped programs for mild-ish politics.  And to be honest I enjoyed it more.  It's probably anathema to a lot of people but I had always felt that development has to be for *someone*, and sometimes the tech gets in the way of the people.

And so here we are, a moment in time when the tech and the people are on more of a collision course than ever.  I'm drawing close to the end of my corporate career but I'm still very much in the mix, and when the mortgage is paid I'll be focusing on what matters to me.  So what's left for the hat?

We will see :-)
